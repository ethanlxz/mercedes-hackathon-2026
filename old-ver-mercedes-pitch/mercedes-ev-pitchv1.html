Problem Statement

Cars collect data, but still drive like strangers.

Lack of PersonalizationCurrent systems treat every driver the same and do not learn individual habits, leading to inaccurate and irrelevant recommendations.
Data is Collected but Not Used WiselyModern cars already collect a lot of data, including driving style, battery usage, and location patterns, but this data is rarely used to help the driver in real time.
Isolated Vehicle SystemsModern cars have many smart features, but they operate in isolation instead of working together, resulting in an inefficient and disconnected driving experience.

The missing piece is coordination.

The car has data, sensors, and smart features. What it lacks is one intelligence layer that turns them into timely, personal help.

Feature 1
05 / 09
Natural-language route planning

Say the journey. The agent builds it.

The driver gives one instruction. The planner parses goals, resolves places, ranks candidates, and computes a route.

Supports cafes, errands, specific places, and time constraints.
Registers the active route for later safety and charging decisions.
Frontend UI: Trip Planner
Feature 2
06 / 09
Fatigue-aware intervention

Not just an alarm. A route-aware next step.

When fatigue rises, the agent searches ahead on the active route and proposes a rest stop the driver can accept with one tap.

Severity changes ranking: closer stops for higher risk.
Preference memory favors stops the driver tends to accept.
Frontend UI: Fatigue Alert
Feature 3
07 / 09
Battery and charging intelligence

Range anxiety becomes a plan.

The agent estimates battery demand, checks reserve, and recommends whether to charge before, during, or near the destination.

Three XGBoost models learn trip distance and weekday usage patterns.
Charging can be bundled with rest stops to reduce total interruptions.
Frontend UI: Battery Planner
